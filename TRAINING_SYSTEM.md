## 9. Sunday: Complete Rest

No required workout. No guilt-driven conditioning. No requirement to close rings or maintain a streak. Walking and ordinary life activity are fine.

## 10. Hypertrophy Execution Rules

The athlete is still improving lifting technique. Priority order: stability, then range of motion, then control, then load.

General tempo guidance: concentric approximately 1-2 seconds, eccentric approximately 2-3 seconds. Use controlled, repeatable technique.

A dramatic muscle sensation is not required for hypertrophy, but exercises should be performed in a manner that appropriately loads the intended musculature.

## 11. RIR (Reps In Reserve)

Examples: 4 RIR means approximately four additional reps possible. 2 RIR means approximately two additional reps possible. 1 RIR means approximately one additional rep possible. 0 RIR means no additional repetition possible with appropriate technique.

Most hypertrophy work should generally finish around 1-3 RIR. Failure is not required on every set.

## 12. Double Progression

Primary strength/hypertrophy progression method: double progression.

Example. Prescription: 3 x 6-10.

Session A: 25 x 9, 25 x 8, 25 x 7.

Next exposure: attempt to improve repetitions while preserving technique.

Eventually: 25 x 10, 25 x 10, 25 x 10. Then increase load and return toward the lower portion of the rep range.

Do not sacrifice technique merely to achieve progression.

## 13. Rest Periods

| Type | Rest |
|---|---|
| Heavy compound exercises | Approximately 2-3 minutes |
| Moderate compound exercises | Approximately 90-150 seconds |
| Isolation exercises | Approximately 60-120 seconds |
| Sprinting | Long enough to preserve speed quality |
| Jumps | Long enough to preserve explosiveness |
| Calisthenics skill | Long enough to produce high-quality attempts |

The app may provide timers, but timers should remain optional.

## 14. Abs

Abs are a high-priority physique goal. Train them like other muscles: resistance, controlled ROM, progressive overload, measurable progression.

Primary movements currently include: cable crunch, hanging knee/leg raise, ab wheel, hollow-body work, L-sit work.

Avoid replacing this with arbitrary high-repetition "ab burner" circuits.

## 15. Athleticism Rules

Muscle gain must coexist with athletic development. The program deliberately maintains:

| Quality | Source |
|---|---|
| Sprint exposure | Tuesday + Ultimate |
| Power exposure | Friday + Ultimate |
| Change of direction | Ultimate |
| Relative strength | Pull-ups, dips, calisthenics |
| Mobility | Integrated throughout the week |
| Conditioning | Ultimate + optional Zone 2 |

The athlete does not need additional random "functional" exercises merely to appear athletic. Stable hypertrophy exercises are useful for building muscle. Dedicated athletic exercises are useful for developing athletic qualities. Each tool should serve its intended purpose.

## 16. Speed Philosophy

Speed training is not conditioning. Speed repetitions should occur relatively fresh, with high intent, excellent technique, adequate recovery, and low/moderate volume. Do not turn sprint training into repeated exhausted running. Ultimate already provides substantial repeated-sprint conditioning.

## 17. Power Philosophy

Power work occurs before heavy strength work. Jumps should be explosive, technically sound, and relatively low volume. Stop or reduce volume when performance meaningfully deteriorates.

## 18. Relative Strength

Bodyweight gain is not automatically beneficial. The objective is more muscle, substantially greater strength, and maintained or improved speed and power, rather than more mass regardless of performance.

Important benchmarks include: strict pull-ups, dips, sprint performance, jumping, calisthenics.

## 19. Athletic Benchmarking

Approximately every 4-6 weeks, consider measuring:

| Benchmark | Quality measured |
|---|---|
| 10 m sprint | Acceleration |
| 20 m sprint | Acceleration |
| 30 m sprint | Speed |
| Standing broad jump | Horizontal power |
| Vertical jump | Vertical power |
| Strict pull-ups | Relative pulling strength |
| Strict dips | Relative pushing strength |
| L-sit | Body control / compression strength |
| Planche progression | Straight-arm strength and skill |

Benchmark testing should not unnecessarily interfere with normal training.

## 20. Adductor / Groin Management

The program includes gradual strengthening through movements such as: Copenhagen plank, adductor machine, unilateral leg work, glute work, controlled mobility.

The software must distinguish ordinary training soreness from pain or recurring aggravation. The software must not diagnose the cause of groin symptoms. If symptoms worsen, persist, affect sprinting, affect cutting, or produce significant pain, recommend assessment by an appropriate sports medicine professional or physical therapist.

## 21. Program Adjustment Philosophy

Do not modify the training plan simply because one workout was poor. Look for trends.

Potential reasons for modification: persistent poor recovery, declining performance, repeated inability to complete prescribed volume, worsening pain, Ultimate workload changes, athletic benchmark regression, sustained strength plateau, schedule changes.

The program should evolve based on evidence.

## 22. Future HYROX Phase

HYROX is intentionally excluded from current specific programming. A future training phase can introduce: compromised running, sled work, HYROX stations, event-specific pacing, race-specific conditioning.

Do not introduce these simply because HYROX is a future goal. The current block develops useful prerequisites.
